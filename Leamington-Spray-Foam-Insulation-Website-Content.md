# Spray Foam Insulation In Leamington, Ontario

## Insulation Built For The Warmest, Dampest Corner Of Canada

Leamington sits further south than any other town on the Canadian mainland. We are on the 42nd parallel, the same line as Chicago and Rome, and Lake Erie wraps around three sides of us. That gives us the longest warm season in Ontario and some of the highest humidity in the country.

It also means the [insulation advice](https://seattleinsulationcompany.com/) written for Barrie or Sudbury does not fit here. Most of it is about holding heat in through a long frozen winter. Half of our problem is the opposite, and it runs from May to September.

We install [spray foam insulation](http://conroesprayfoaminsulation.com/) in attics, walls, basements, crawl spaces, garages, greenhouses and packhouses across Leamington and south Essex County. Every job starts with somebody looking at the space.

## Why Summer Is The Season That Decides Your Insulation Here

Point Pelee averages more than 170 frost-free days a year. Guelph, a couple of hours up the road, gets about 135. That is more than a month of extra warm weather, and it comes with lake humidity sitting on top of it.

When it is 30 degrees and sticky outside and your basement is at 20, the moisture in that outdoor air wants to move inward. It travels through block and brick and finds the first cool surface it can reach. If that surface is the back of a fibreglass batt against a foundation wall, the water condenses there, out of sight, and stays.

That is why so many Leamington basements smell musty in August rather than in January. It is not a leak. It is warm wet air meeting a cold wall inside a stud bay nobody can see into.

[Closed cell spray foam](http://kirklandsprayfoaminsulation.com) sprayed straight onto the concrete removes the gap where that happens. There is no cavity for the air to reach and no cold face for it to condense on. This is the single biggest difference between insulating a house here and insulating one two hours north.

## What Are The Benefits Of Spray Foam Insulation?

It seals and insulates in the same pass. Foam expands into the gaps around joists, wiring, plumbing and top plates, which is where most of the air actually moves.

Closed cell foam is its own vapour control layer at the right thickness. In a humid climate that matters more than the R-value printed on the bag.

It holds its shape. Batts sag, settle and get pushed aside. Foam is stuck to the substrate and stays where it was sprayed.

It does not feed mould and it is not a nesting material. In a crawl space near Hillman Marsh that is worth a great deal.

Closed cell foam adds racking strength to a wall assembly. In the most tornado-prone part of Canada that is a genuine side benefit.

It fits odd shapes. Rim joists, brick ledges, the angle where a knee wall meets a roof deck, the top of a foundation wall. Batts never fit those properly.

## Closed Cell Or Open Cell In A Leamington House

The short rule we use: closed cell anywhere the moisture is on the other side, open cell where it is not.

Closed cell goes on foundation walls, crawl space walls, rim joists, floors over unheated space, and anything in an agricultural or cold storage building. It runs about R-6 to R-7 an inch and stops vapour once it is thick enough.

Open cell is for interior partitions, ceilings under a conditioned attic, and rooms where you want the sound knocked down. It is around R-3.5 to R-3.8 an inch and costs less, but it does not control vapour at all.

Getting this backwards is the most common mistake we are called out to fix. Open cell on a Leamington basement wall lets summer humidity straight through to the concrete, and then you have a damp wall behind new drywall.

## The Clay Under Your House Changes The Job

Essex County sits on thirty to sixty metres of clay over limestone. It holds water rather than draining it, and the water table around Leamington is high and close to the surface.

Two things follow from that. Foundation walls here stay damp on the outside for most of the year, so whatever you put against them on the inside has to be able to cope with that. And the ground around your footings moves as the clay swells and shrinks, which opens small gaps at the rim joist over time.

We take a moisture reading on the concrete before we spray anything. If the slab or the wall is wet, foam is the wrong first purchase and we will say so. Grading, downspouts and weeping tile come first. Insulation goes on after.

## Crawl Spaces Near The Lake And The Marsh

South of town, out toward Point Pelee, Marentette Beach, Sturgeon Woods and the Hillman area, a lot of houses sit on crawl spaces rather than full basements. Much of that ground was marsh before it was drained.

Those crawl spaces were nearly all built vented, on the theory that outside air would dry them out. In a climate this humid it does the opposite. You are pulling damp lake air into a cool space under the floor all summer, and it condenses on the joists.

The fix is to stop treating the crawl space as outdoors. Seal the vents, sheet the ground, spray the perimeter walls and the rim, and let the space sit at the same conditions as the house above it. Floors get warmer, the musty smell goes, and the joists stop taking on water every July.

## Attic Insulation

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does my basement smell worse in summer than in winter?

Because the problem is humidity, not cold. Leamington has some of the dampest summer air in Ontario. That warm wet air moves inward through the foundation and condenses on the first cool surface it reaches, which is usually the back of the insulation against the concrete.

Winter dries a house out. Summer loads it up. If the smell is seasonal and it peaks in July and August, you are looking at inward vapour drive rather than a leak.

### Can spray foam fix a basement that takes water?

It cannot. Foam controls air and vapour. It is not a waterproofing product, standing water under pressure goes straight past it, and it replaces nothing that drainage does.

Where water is still coming in, the outside gets sorted first. Ground falling away from the wall, downspouts discharging well clear, weeping tile actually running. Insulation comes after that. We will decline a job rather than bury a leak behind foam.

### Do I have to cover spray foam with drywall?

In an occupied space, yes. The code requires a thermal barrier over exposed foam and half-inch drywall is the standard way to do it. In an attic or crawl space that is not normally occupied, a thinner tested ignition barrier is often acceptable instead.

Get this priced into the job at the start. It is one of the more common surprises on a cheap quote.

### How long does the smell last after installation?

Properly mixed foam is cured and off-gassing has stopped by the time we hand the space back, usually within a day for residential work. We will give you a re-occupancy time for your specific job before we start.

A chemical smell that hangs around for weeks is a sign of off-ratio foam, which means the two components were not mixed correctly. That does not fix itself and it cannot be sprayed over. It has to come out.

### Can you insulate a greenhouse?

Not the glazing, no, and anyone offering to is not doing you a favour. What we insulate on a greenhouse site is the headhouse, the packing and grading areas, cold storage, boiler rooms, north gable ends and the perimeter curtain wall below bench height.

Those are the parts that lose heat without costing you light, and on a heated range the perimeter work in particular pays back quickly.

## Attic Insulation

An attic is the cheapest comfort you can buy in most Leamington houses, and it is usually the first thing we look at. Warm indoor air rises, finds every gap in the ceiling, and takes moisture with it on the way out.

## Vented Attic Or Sealed Attic

If your roof has soffit vents, a ridge vent and nothing mechanical in the attic, the usual answer is to air seal and insulate the attic floor and leave the roof breathing as designed.

If there is ducting or an air handler up there, or the roof is a cathedral or low-slope with no room to vent properly, sealing the attic into the house is often the better answer. Foam goes on the underside of the deck, the vents are closed off, and the attic becomes part of the conditioned building.

Both work. Doing half of one and half of the other does not, and we see that fairly often on houses where somebody has added foam in patches over the years.

In most of Ontario an attic upgrade is sold on winter heating. Here it earns at least as much in August.

A dark roof on a still July afternoon in Leamington can run far hotter than the air temperature. Depth is the number people ask about. Gaps are the number that matters.

Bathroom fans that vent into the attic instead of outside. The chase around a chimney or a plumbing stack. Pot lights. The top plate of every interior wall. The hatch itself, which is often a sheet of plywood with a batt loose on top of it.

Air sealing those is a large part of the job and it is where most of the improvement comes from. A very deep layer of insulation with a hole in the middle of it is still a hole.

## Basement Insulation

A Leamington basement has two things working against it. It is surrounded by clay that holds water, and it spends four months of the year with warm humid air pressing in from outside.

Closed cell spray foam applied straight onto the foundation wall deals with both at once. It insulates, it seals the air, and at the right thickness it is its own vapour control layer.

## The Batt And Poly Wall Is The One That Fails Here

This is the assembly we are called out to replace more than any other.

Studs get framed a couple of inches off the foundation, batts go in between them, poly goes over the front, drywall goes on top. It looks finished. Behind it there is a gap of still air against cold damp concrete.

In our summers, humid air finds that gap through the block and condenses on the concrete face. The batt gets damp, the poly holds the water in, and nothing dries. The first sign is usually a smell in August, and by the time it is visible the drywall has to come off.

Foam removes the gap. There is nothing behind it for air to reach.

## The Code Number Moved A Long Way

The Ontario Building Code now asks for R-20 on basement walls in new construction. It was R-12. That change alone reset what a basement wall has to be.

Closed cell gets you there in a manageable thickness while doing the air and vapour work at the same time. Getting to R-20 with batts alone means a much thicker wall and a lot more careful detailing to avoid exactly the problem above.

## Rim Joists Come With The Job

The rim joist is the band of wood sitting on top of your foundation wall, right where the floor structure starts. It is thin, it is exposed on the outside, and it is full of gaps.

It is a small area doing a very large amount of the leaking in most houses, and it is normally the cheapest square footage on a basement quote. We include it as standard.

## Water Comes First, Always

If your basement takes water, insulation is not the first purchase. We will take moisture readings on the walls and slab before quoting, and if the readings say drainage, we will tell you.

Ground falling away from the wall, downspouts discharging well clear, weeping tile actually running. Sort that first. Foam over a live leak hides the problem behind something you would have to cut out to reach, and we will not install it that way.

# Crawl Space Insulation In Leamington, Ontario

## Crawl Space Insulation

South of Leamington, out toward Point Pelee, Marentette Beach, Sturgeon Woods and the Hillman area, a lot of houses sit over crawl spaces instead of full basements. Much of that land was marsh before it was drained, and the water table is close under your feet.

Those crawl spaces cause more comfort complaints than anything else we see locally, and almost all of them come down to the same design assumption.

## Vented Crawl Spaces Do Not Work Here

Nearly every older crawl space in the area was built with vents in the perimeter wall. The idea was that outside air moving through would keep the space dry.

That works in a dry climate. In the most humid corner of Ontario it does the opposite. All summer you are drawing warm damp lake air into a cool space under the floor, where it hits joists and ductwork and condenses on them.

The result is the smell coming up through the floor, cold damp floorboards over the crawl space, and joists that take on moisture every year.

## Sealed, Not Vented

The modern approach is to stop treating the crawl space as outdoors and make it part of the house.

The vents get closed and sealed. The ground gets a heavy sheet, lapped and taped and run up the walls. Closed cell foam goes on the perimeter walls and the rim joist rather than between the floor joists above. The space then sits at roughly the same temperature and humidity as the rooms above it.

Floors get noticeably warmer, the smell goes, and the framing stops cycling wet and dry through the year.

## Access Is Most Of The Cost

A crawl space quote is driven more by headroom and hatch size than by square footage.

Eighteen inches of clearance, a hatch through a closet floor, and ducting in the way is a slow, physical day for a crew. The same area in a four-foot crawl with an exterior door is a different job at a different price. We would rather see it than guess at it.

## What We Check Before Quoting

Standing water or a wet ground sheet. Existing insulation hanging out of the joists, which is a sign it has been wet. Rodent activity. Whether the ducting up there is sealed. Whether there is a sump and whether it works.

If the space is actively wet, that gets fixed before any foam goes in.

# New Construction Insulation In Leamington, Ontario

## New Construction Insulation

If you are building in Leamington, the insulation decision has changed since the 2024 code came into force. The R-values went up and, more importantly, the emphasis moved toward airtightness and whole-building performance.

Spray foam suits that shift because it does the air sealing and the insulating in one operation rather than relying on a separate membrane installed perfectly.

## What Changed In The 2024 Code

Under the current SB-12 numbers for this zone, a new build with high efficiency heating is looking at around R-50 for a ceiling with an attic, R-31 for a ceiling without one, R-24 above grade walls and R-20 for basement walls.

Basement walls nearly doubled. That is the headline change and it is the one that most often sends a builder toward foam, because getting to R-20 with batts means a thicker wall and much more careful detailing.

Beyond the R-values, the code now weighs measured air leakage far more heavily than it used to. An assembly can hit its nominal R-value and still fail a blower door test.

## Flash And Batt, And When It Makes Sense

You do not always need a full cavity of foam. Flash and batt puts a controlled layer of closed cell against the sheathing to handle the air and vapour work, then fills the rest of the cavity with batt.

It is often the sensible middle ground on above grade walls: you get the sealing and the condensation control where it matters, without paying to fill the whole depth with foam.

Below grade and at rim joists we would still go full closed cell.

## Where Foam Earns Its Place Outright

Rim joists, cantilevers, the floor over an unheated garage, brick ledges, and any wall assembly that has to hit a blower door target. Those are the places where nothing else does the job as reliably.

On a Leamington site there is one more. Closed cell bonded to the sheathing adds real racking strength, and this is the most tornado-prone corner of the country.

## Fitting Into The Schedule

We come in after rough-in and inspection and before drywall. That window is usually tight, so booking us at framing stage rather than the week before drywall is worth doing.

We will also tell you at quote stage what thermal barrier the code will want over the foam, so it is in the budget rather than a surprise.

## Insulation Removal

Insulation does not last forever, and in a damp climate it often does not last as long as people expect. When the existing material is wet, mouldy, rodent damaged or flattened, it has to come out before anything new goes in.

We remove attic, wall, basement and crawl space insulation and dispose of it properly. It is quoted as its own job so you can see exactly what that part is costing.

## Why We Will Not Just Spray Over It

Foam bonds to the surface it is sprayed onto. Spray it onto a damp compressed batt and you have not fixed anything. You have sealed a wet problem inside an assembly where nobody can inspect it.

The same goes for rodent contaminated material. Sealing that in does not make it go away, and you will smell it in July.

If the existing insulation is dry, intact and doing its job, we will tell you it can stay. We would rather do a smaller job properly than sell you a removal you do not need.

## What Tells Us It Has To Go

Batts hanging out of crawl space joists. That is almost always a sign the material has been wet and has lost its loft.

Grey, flattened or matted attic insulation. Droppings, runs or nesting material. A musty smell that gets worse in warm weather.

Water staining on the underside of the roof deck. Insulation packed tight against a soffit vent so the roof cannot breathe.

Anything that was installed over a leak that has since been fixed. It will still be holding the water.

## Old Foam That Failed

We also remove spray foam that was installed badly in the first place. Off-ratio foam, where the two components were not mixed correctly, does not cure properly. It shrinks, pulls away from the framing, sometimes crumbles, and it can keep giving off a chemical smell for months.

There is no way to fix it in place and no way to spray over it. It has to be cut back to clean substrate and redone. If you have foam that still smells, or that has visibly pulled away from a rafter, get somebody to look at it.

The area gets sealed off from the rest of the house and put under negative pressure so nothing travels. Material is vacuumed out or bagged at source rather than carried through the building. Surfaces get cleaned down and, where needed, treated before anything new goes on.

Then we take a moisture reading on the bare substrate. That reading decides whether the new insulation goes in that week or whether something else has to be fixed first.

# Commercial And Cold Storage Insulation In Leamington, Ontario

## Commercial and Cold Storage Insulation

Leamington runs on food. Processing, packing, refrigerated storage and the buildings that support them, from the Highbury Canco plant through to the shops and warehouses along Erie Street and out on the Talbot Street corridor.

Those buildings have a different insulation problem to a house. They are usually large, often steel framed, and frequently holding a temperature that is a long way from the outside air.

## Cold Storage Is A Vapour Problem First

A refrigerated room in a Leamington summer has the largest vapour drive of any building in town. Outside it is thirty degrees and humid. Inside it is a few degrees above freezing. That difference pushes water toward the cold side constantly.

If it gets into the assembly it condenses, then freezes. Once ice forms in an insulation layer it stops insulating and starts damaging the structure around it.

Closed cell foam is a continuous air and vapour barrier as well as an insulation layer, which is why it is used so widely in this application. There is no seam for the vapour to find.

## Steel Buildings And Thermal Bridging

A steel frame conducts heat very well, which is the last thing you want in a wall. Batts between girts leave the steel itself bridging straight through.

Foam sprayed over the inside face of the assembly covers the framing as well as the space between it. That continuous layer is worth more than the nominal R-value suggests, because it deals with the bridging rather than working around it.

## Where It Goes On A Commercial Site

Roof decks and underside of steel deck. Perimeter walls and gables. Loading dock surrounds, which leak badly and are usually ignored. Rim conditions at mezzanine floors. Refrigerated room envelopes. Boiler and mechanical rooms where you are trying to keep heat in a small space.

## Covering, Fire Rating And Occupancy

Commercial work brings its own requirements around thermal barriers, ignition barriers and flame spread ratings, and they are stricter than residential in occupied areas. We spray material meeting CAN/ULC S705.1 and install to S705.2, and we will spell out the covering requirement in the quote rather than leaving it for the inspection.

We also work around production. Most of this gets done in a shutdown window or overnight, and that gets planned before the quote is signed.

# Greenhouse And Agricultural Insulation In Leamington, Ontario

## Greenhouse and Agricultural Insulation

There are close to two thousand acres of commercial greenhouse around Leamington, the largest concentration anywhere in North America. Add the barns, shops and storage buildings across Mersea and out toward Blytheswood and Albuna, and agriculture is most of what gets built here.

We insulate the parts of those operations that should be insulated, and we are straight with growers about the parts that should not be.

## We Do Not Insulate Glazing

Nobody sprays foam on glass or poly. The whole point of the structure is light transmission and foam is opaque. If somebody offers to insulate your growing area with spray foam, that is a reason to stop the conversation.

Roof heat loss on a modern range is handled by energy curtains, and that is the right tool for it.

## What Does Get Insulated

Headhouses, grading lines and packing rooms, which are usually heated harder than the range itself and are often barely insulated at all.

Cold storage and shipping areas, where the vapour drive in a Leamington August is severe.

Boiler rooms and mechanical spaces, and the pipe runs through unheated areas where the heat is not doing anything useful.

North gable ends, which get almost no useful light and lose heat all winter.

Perimeter curtain walls below bench height. Once the curtains are handling the roof, the walls become a disproportionate share of the total loss, and this is usually the shortest payback on the site.

## Barns, Shops And Storage

Closed cell foam sprayed to the underside of a steel roof deck stops the condensation drip that ruins stored equipment and feed. On a barn that is often the main reason for doing it rather than the heat saving.

Pole frame shops, machinery storage and workshop conversions all take foam well, and the irregular framing that makes batts awkward is not a problem for it.

For livestock buildings the washdown durability and the fact that foam gives rodents nothing to nest in both matter as much as the R-value.

## What A Grower Should Ask For

A scope that separates the work by payback rather than pricing the whole site as one number. We would rather do the perimeter and the packhouse this year and leave the rest than sell you everything at once.

We will also give you a written spec with the product and the installed thickness, which matters if you are putting the work through any kind of efficiency program.

## Book A Site Walk

Tell us the range size, how it is heated and which buildings are on the site. We will walk it and come back with a scope in order of what pays back first.

# About Leamington Spray Foam Insulation

## Built Around Leamington Houses And Leamington Weather

We are an insulation contractor working in Leamington and south Essex County. That is the whole service area. We are not driving down from London or across from another county to fit you in.

It is not a small distinction. Buildings in this county fail in the same handful of ways, and after enough of them you stop guessing and start recognising. Clay that holds water. A high water table. Crawl spaces on ground that used to be marsh. And four months a year when the outdoor air carries more moisture than anywhere else in the province.

## Why We Talk About Summer So Much

Most insulation companies sell winter. Heat loss, drafts, furnace bills. All of that is real here too.

But Leamington is the warmest place in Canada with the longest frost-free season, and a good share of the failures we are called out to look at happen in July rather than January. Damp foundation walls behind new drywall. Musty crawl spaces. Batts that have quietly taken on water for years.

Every one of those is warm humid air moving inward and finding somewhere cool to condense. If a quote you are comparing does not mention vapour at all, it was written for a different climate.

## Ask Whoever Quotes You Which Standard They Spray To

Spray foam is a regulated product with a real standard behind it. Material has to meet CAN/ULC S705.1 and installation has to meet CAN/ULC S705.2. CUFCA is the recognised certifying body in Canada.

Very few homeowners know to ask about this, and very few competitor sites mention it. Ask it of us and ask it of everyone else quoting the job. The answer tells you a lot.

The other thing worth asking is what happens to the foam afterwards. It cannot be left exposed in an occupied space. It needs a thermal barrier over it, and half-inch drywall is the standard assembly. That is a real cost and it belongs in the quote from the start.

## How We Quote

Somebody comes out and looks at the space. We take moisture readings on the concrete or the substrate and we check what the existing insulation is doing.

You get a written quote naming the product, the installed thickness and the price. Not a square foot rate and not a range. If the code will want drywall or an ignition barrier over the foam, that is on the same page.

The number does not move afterwards unless you change the scope. If we find something once the space is opened up, we stop and talk to you about it rather than adding it to the invoice.

## Four Things We Talk People Out Of

Spraying foam over an active leak. Where the meter points at drainage, drainage is the answer, and we will hand the job back.

Removing insulation that is dry and doing its job. If it is fine, leave it.

Open cell foam on a basement or crawl space wall. In this climate that is a slow moving problem you will pay to fix twice.

Foam on greenhouse glazing. It is opaque. The whole building is for light.

# FAQ SECTION

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does spray foam insulation cost in Leamington?

It depends on the product, the installed thickness and how hard the space is to work in. Across Ontario in 2026, open cell generally runs somewhere around one and a half to two and a half dollars a square foot, and closed cell roughly three to seven. Those are market ranges rather than a quote for your building.

Getting to the work drives the total harder than the foam does. A basement you can stand up in and a crawl space with eighteen inches of headroom might swallow the same amount of material, but one of them takes a crew twice as long on their backs.

There is a floor on what we can quote. Getting a rig on site, setting up and opening material costs the same whether the area is large or small, so very small jobs carry a minimum. You hear that figure before anything is booked.

### Why do so many Leamington basements smell in summer?

Because we have the most humid summers in Ontario and a lot of basements were insulated with a gap behind the batts.

Warm outdoor air carries a great deal of moisture in July and August. It moves inward through block and brick, reaches the cool concrete behind the insulation, and condenses there. The batt gets damp, a poly sheet on the warm side holds the water in, and nothing dries out until the weather changes.

Closed cell foam sprayed directly onto the concrete removes the gap entirely. There is nothing behind it for the air to reach.

### Can spray foam fix a basement that takes water?

It cannot, and an installer who tells you otherwise wants the sale more than the outcome. Foam controls air and vapour. Standing water under pressure goes straight past it, and it replaces nothing that drainage does.

Where water is still coming in, the outside gets sorted first. Ground falling away from the wall, downspouts discharging well clear, weeping tile actually running. Insulation comes after that. We will decline a job rather than bury a leak behind foam.

### Should my crawl space be vented or sealed?

Sealed, in almost every case around here. The vents were put in on the assumption that outside air would dry the space out, and in this climate they do the opposite for most of the year.

Closing the vents, sheeting the ground and insulating the perimeter walls brings the crawl space inside the building envelope. Floors above get warmer and the framing stops taking on moisture every summer.

### Does spray foam have to be covered with drywall?

In occupied space, yes. The Ontario Building Code requires a thermal barrier over exposed foam, and half-inch gypsum drywall is the standard accepted assembly. Approved spray-applied intumescent coatings are an alternative.

Where a space is not normally lived in, an attic or a crawl space for instance, a tested ignition barrier of lesser thickness will usually satisfy the requirement.

Most homeowners do not know this and a lot of cheap quotes leave it out. Ask about it before you compare prices.

### How long does the smell last after installation?

For properly mixed foam, curing is done and off-gassing has stopped by the time we hand the space back. For most residential work that is the same day, and we give you a re-occupancy time for your specific job before we start.

A chemical smell that persists for weeks or months means off-ratio foam, where the two components were not mixed in the right proportion. That does not cure out and it cannot be sprayed over. It has to be cut back to clean substrate and redone.

### What is the difference between open cell and closed cell?

Closed cell is rigid, runs about R-6 to R-7 an inch, and at sufficient thickness it is its own vapour barrier. It goes below grade, on crawl space walls, on rim joists and in agricultural and cold storage buildings.

Open cell is soft, runs about R-3.5 to R-3.8 an inch, costs less, and does not control vapour at all. It suits interior partitions, sound damping and ceilings under conditioned space.

In Leamington the moisture question decides it more often than the R-value does.

### Is there a rebate available?

The Home Renovation Savings Program is the current one, delivered by Save on Energy with Enbridge Gas, and it is scheduled to run to 30 November 2026.

There is a standalone attic insulation stream that does not require an energy assessment, and a larger multi-measure stream that does require both a pre-work and a post-work assessment. These two get confused constantly, and being sent for an audit when you only want the attic done wastes your time and money.

We are not the program administrator, so we do not quote amounts. We will tell you which stream your job fits and where to check the current terms.

### Can you insulate a greenhouse?

Not the glazing. Foam is opaque and the structure exists to let light in.

What we do insulate on a greenhouse site is the headhouse, packing and grading rooms, cold storage, boiler rooms, north gable ends and the perimeter curtain wall below bench height. On a heated range the perimeter work usually has the shortest payback of anything on the property.

### Do you remove old insulation?

Yes, and it is quoted as a separate job so you can see what that part costs. Wet, mouldy, rodent damaged or flattened material has to come out before new insulation goes in.

We will not spray over failed insulation. Foam bonds to whatever is underneath it, so spraying over a damp batt seals the problem into the assembly rather than fixing it.

If the existing insulation is dry and intact, we will tell you it can stay.
