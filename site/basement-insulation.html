Basement Insulation

Basement Insulation In Leamington, Ontario

A Leamington basement has two things working against it. It is surrounded by clay that holds water, and it spends four months of the year with warm humid air pressing in from outside.

Closed cell spray foam applied straight onto the foundation wall deals with both at once. It insulates, it seals the air, and at the right thickness it is its own vapour control layer.

Basement Insulation details

The Batt And Poly Wall Is The One That Fails Here

This is the assembly we are called out to replace more than any other.

Studs get framed a couple of inches off the foundation, batts go in between them, poly goes over the front, drywall goes on top. It looks finished. Behind it there is a gap of still air against cold damp concrete.

In our summers, humid air finds that gap through the block and condenses on the concrete face. The batt gets damp, the poly holds the water in, and nothing dries. The first sign is usually a smell in August, and by the time it is visible the drywall has to come off.

Foam removes the gap. There is nothing behind it for air to reach.

The Code Number Moved A Long Way

The Ontario Building Code now asks for R-20 on basement walls in new construction. It was R-12. That change alone reset what a basement wall has to be.

Closed cell gets you there in a manageable thickness while doing the air and vapour work at the same time. Getting to R-20 with batts alone means a much thicker wall and a lot more careful detailing to avoid exactly the problem above.

Rim Joists Come With The Job

The rim joist is the band of wood sitting on top of your foundation wall, right where the floor structure starts. It is thin, it is exposed on the outside, and it is full of gaps.

It is a small area doing a very large amount of the leaking in most houses, and it is normally the cheapest square footage on a basement quote. We include it as standard.

Water Comes First, Always

If your basement takes water, insulation is not the first purchase. We will take moisture readings on the walls and slab before quoting, and if the readings say drainage, we will tell you.

Ground falling away from the wall, downspouts discharging well clear, weeping tile actually running. Sort that first. Foam over a live leak hides the problem behind something you would have to cut out to reach, and we will not install it that way.
