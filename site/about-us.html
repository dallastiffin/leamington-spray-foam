We Talk About Vapour More Than Most

Most insulation companies sell winter. Heat loss, drafts, furnace bills. All of that is real.

But a good share of the failures we are called to look at happen in July rather than January. Damp foundation walls behind new drywall. Musty crawl spaces. Batts that have quietly taken on water for years.

Every one of those is warm humid air moving inward and condensing on something cool. Being this close to Lake Erie makes it more pronounced, but the mechanism is the same everywhere. If a quote you are comparing does not mention vapour control at all, ask why.

Ask Whoever Quotes You Which Standard They Spray To

Spray foam is a regulated product with a real standard behind it. Material has to meet CAN/ULC S705.1 and installation has to meet CAN/ULC S705.2. CUFCA is the recognised certifying body in Canada.

Very few homeowners know to ask about this, and very few competitor sites mention it. Ask it of us and ask it of everyone else quoting the job. The answer tells you a lot.

The other thing worth asking is what happens to the foam afterwards. It cannot be left exposed in an occupied space. It needs a thermal barrier over it, and half-inch drywall is the standard assembly. That is a real cost and it belongs in the quote from the start.

How We Quote

Somebody comes out and looks at the space. We take moisture readings on the concrete or the substrate and we check what the existing insulation is doing.

You get a written quote naming the product, the installed thickness and the price. Not a square foot rate and not a range. If the code will want drywall or an ignition barrier over the foam, that is on the same page.

The number does not move afterwards unless you change the scope. If we find something once the space is opened up, we stop and talk to you about it rather than adding it to the invoice.

Four Things We Talk People Out Of

Spraying foam over an active leak. Where the meter points at drainage, drainage is the answer, and we will hand the job back.

Removing insulation that is dry and doing its job. If it is fine, leave it.

Open cell foam on a basement or crawl space wall. In this climate that is a slow moving problem you will pay to fix twice.

Foam on greenhouse glazing. It is opaque. The whole building is for light.
